Ask for the address in the booking flow and confirm what was inferred

The service area is now two controls in Business details — "Limit service
area?" and a distance picked from a list — instead of a per-question type
dropdown in the Booking section. That section is the owner's own wording,
and asking them to mark a question's data type in order to turn on a
business rule was the wrong question in the wrong place. It also failed
quietly: the type defaulted to text, so a business could have a service
radius configured and no check running, with nothing anywhere saying so.

Turning the limit on is now the only switch. The address becomes a
built-in booking slot rather than something the owner wires up, asked
second — after the service, before any times — so a customer outside the
area is turned away before describing their problem, picking a slot, or
costing a calendar lookup. An owner who already wrote their own address
question keeps their wording, and the key stays service_address so
existing appointments still read correctly.

Addresses are confirmed only when the geocoder inferred something. "1738
William St, Buffalo NY" resolving to Buffalo is the system agreeing with
the customer; the same street silently resolving to Rochester because
that's where the search was biased is the system deciding on their
behalf, and that decision books a job sixty miles from where someone
lives. An address that can't be placed at all is re-asked twice, then
booked as typed and flagged — a geocoder still can't block a booking.

Fixes two bugs a live booking exposed. The geocode cache stored a
lookup's result but not the reason it failed, so the first miss reported
"address not found" and every cached one reported nothing — which the
booking flow then read as our outage rather than something the customer
could fix, and skipped the re-ask. And a completed check was never
persisted, because handle_booking saves state before the check runs, so
every message in a booking re-checked the same address.
